Academic profile

Dr. Kishor Datta Gupta

Assistant Professor, Clark Atlanta University

Atlanta, Georgia

Dr. Gupta is a tenure-track Assistant Professor whose work focuses on artificial intelligence, machine learning, and cybersecurity. His competitive awards include the 2025 NASA MUREP Partnership Learning Annual Notification award, two Department of Defense research grants, and a Nuclear Regulatory Commission award.

He has served as a Visiting Faculty Researcher at the Air Force Research Laboratory and has advanced work in multimodal data fusion, physics-guided neural networks, trustworthy AI, and autonomous systems. He is an IEEE Senior Member with journal and conference publications, editorial service, research leadership, and a record of mentoring students toward national and international achievements.

Clark Atlanta University

Teaching

Courses in computer vision, machine learning, operating systems, and advanced algorithms, combining foundations with practical and research-oriented work.

CPS/CS XXXX

Computer Vision: From OpenCV to Vision-Language and World Models

Hybrid, laboratory-centered course covering OpenCV, CNNs, YOLO, ResNet, vision transformers, vision-language models, and predictive world models. Each assignment includes a GitHub repository and narrated demo video workflow.

View course syllabus
CCIS 434

Machine Learning

Introduces supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning. Students use Python, scikit-learn, and Google Colab to analyze data and develop predictive models.

CCIS 634

Advanced Machine Learning

Covers contemporary deep-learning and AI methods, including CNNs, RNNs, transformers, and generative models, with theoretical and implementation work across vision, NLP, and multimodal tasks.

CCIS 473

Introduction to Operating Systems

Examines processes, scheduling, memory management, file systems, and virtualization, emphasizing secure and efficient resource management.

CCIS 673

Advanced Operating Systems

Studies distributed systems, concurrency, security, and cloud or edge operating-system design through research-oriented projects.

CCIS 653

Advanced Algorithms

Graduate study of approximation and randomized algorithms, advanced graph theory, network flows, computational geometry, and complexity theory, with applications to AI, data science, and cybersecurity research.

Teaching experience listed on the introductory page: Operating Systems at CAU from 2022 to 2024, and graduate-level Applied Machine Learning at CAU from 2023 to 2024.
