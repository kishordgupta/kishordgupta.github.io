About

Research grounded in real systems.

My work focuses on AI that must remain useful when data are incomplete, models are attacked, computing resources are limited, and decisions affect physical operations.

Dr. Kishor Datta Gupta is a tenure-track Assistant Professor in the Department of Cyber-Physical Systems at Clark Atlanta University.

His research connects artificial intelligence, machine learning, cybersecurity, and cyber-physical systems. Current directions include trustworthy and adversarial AI, physics-guided learning, knowledge graphs, multimodal perception, LLM and VLM evaluation, UAV autonomy, edge AI, robotics, and security for industrial control systems.

He has participated in research awards and collaborations involving AFRL, NASA, the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission, Army Research Laboratory programs, Google-supported initiatives, and HBCU research partnerships. He also brings prior industry experience in mobile systems and software research.

Research

Core areas of inquiry.

The research program combines data-driven learning with physical structure, deterministic knowledge, security analysis, and operational evaluation.

01

Trustworthy and Adversarial AI

Robustness, explainability, attack and defense methods, model monitoring, and dependable evaluation beyond aggregate accuracy.

02

Physics- and Knowledge-Guided Learning

Neural models informed by physical constraints, knowledge graphs, structured rules, and domain evidence.

03

LLM and VLM Evaluation

Continuous assessment of hallucination, bias, drift, robustness, grounding, source use, and response consistency.

04

Multimodal Perception

Fusion of images, language, thermal data, LiDAR, and other sensor signals for retrieval and situational understanding.

05

Autonomy, Edge AI, and Robotics

Resource-aware perception and decision systems for UAVs, robots, VLA pipelines, and world-model-based autonomy.

06

Critical Infrastructure Security

Cybersecurity for industrial control systems, SCADA, nuclear and manufacturing environments, and cyber-physical operations.

Teaching and mentoring

Learning through implementation and research.
